Determination of residency status

check

Before you begin

If you have entered or left Canada, this tool can help you determine your residency status for Canadian income tax purposes, which affects your income tax obligations and filing requirements in Canada.

Note: Your residency status is determined based on the information you provide and may change if your situation changes. If you plan to enter or leave Canada in the future, use this tool at that time to help determine your residency status.


You will need the following information about:
  • Dates you entered or left Canada
  • Your home and family
  • Your work or studies
  • Your connections to Canada and any other country

Short trips outside Canada, such as vacations or holidays, generally do not affect your residency status.

Daily travel within or outside of Canada

Individuals who enter Canada daily to work or study and return to their home country the same day are generally considered non-resident commuters and do not establish residential ties in Canada.

Canadians who travel daily to another country for work or study generally do not sever their residential ties with Canada and remain residents of Canada.

This status was determined because you established significant residential ties with Canada.

Next steps

  1. Print or save this page for your records
  2. Notify the Canada Revenue Agency of your residency status
  3. File or adjust your return for the year you became a resident:
    • If you have not yet prepared your return, file it using your date of entry under the Resdence Information section of your Income Tax and Benefit Return
    • If you have already filed your return without your immigration date, adjust your tax return, go to Changing a tax return.
  4. If necessary, notify any payers in Canada (such as banks, employer, pension payer, and similar) of your change of residence

As a resident of Canada, you must report all the income you earn from anywhere in the world, starting from the date you became a resident. This includes income from both inside and outside Canada.

For the period before you entered Canada and became a resident, you have to report your income from certain Canadian sources on a Canadian income tax return, including:
  • the taxable part of Canadian scholarships, bursaries, and net research grants
  • income from a business, an office, and employment in Canada
  • taxable capital gains from disposing of Canadian property

Deemed resident of Canada

This status was determined because you have not established significant residential ties with Canada. However, you were present in Canada for 183 days or more during the year. Your country of residence does not have a tax treaty with Canada. Therefore, you are a deemed resident for the entire tax year and until your situation changes.

Note: Do not mail or submit this form to the CRA, unless specifically asked.

Next steps

  1. Print or save this page for your records
  2. Notify the Canada Revenue Agency of your residency status
  3. Prepare your return
    • Make sure to indicate your residency status in the Information about your residency status section of the Income Tax and Benefit Return
    • If you already filed your return without this information, adjust your tax return, go to Changing a tax return.
  4. If necessary, notify any payers in Canada (such as banks, employer, pension payer, and similar) of your change of residence

As a deemed resident of Canada, you are subject to tax in Canada on your world income (income from all sources, both inside and outside Canada). For the tax year, you:
  • must report your world income for the entire tax year
  • can claim all deductions and non-refundable tax credits that apply to you
  • are subject to federal tax and instead of paying provincial or territorial tax, you pay a federal surtax
  • can claim all federal tax credits, but you cannot claim provincial or territorial tax credits
  • are eligible to apply for the goods and services tax/harmonized sales tax (GST/HST) credit


    • For more information on your tax obligations, go to Deemed residents of Canada

    If you need to change an assessed income tax and benefit return to reflect your residency status, go to Changing a tax return.

Non-resident of Canada

This status was determined because you have not established significant residential ties with Canada.

Note: Do not mail or submit this form to the CRA, unless specifically asked.

Next steps

  1. Print or save this page for your records
  2. Notify the Canada Revenue Agency of your residency status
  3. Prepare your return
    • Make sure to indicate your residency status in the Information about your residency status section of the Income Tax and Benefit Return
    • If you have already filed your return without this information, adjust your tax return, go to Changing a tax return.
  4. If necessary, notify any payers in Canada (such as banks, employer, pension payer, and similar) of your change of residence

As a non-resident, you are subject to Canadian tax only on income you receive from Canadian sources.

Generally, a non-resident's Canadian-source income is subject to either Part XIII tax (such as interest, dividends, rent, and pensions) or Part I tax (such as employment income earned in Canada, business income from Canada, and gains from the sale of taxable Canadian property).

If you receive Canadian income that is subject to Part XIII tax:
  • Canadian payers (including financial institutions) must deduct Part XIII tax when income is paid or credited to you
  • The Part XIII tax deducted is your final tax obligation to Canada on this income (if the correct amount is deducted)
  • The usual Part XIII tax rate is 25% unless a tax treaty between Canada and your home country reduces this rate
  • Part XIII tax is not refundable by way of filing a Canadian tax return.


If you receive Canadian income that is subject to Part I tax:
  • The payer usually deducts Part I tax
  • Even if the payer deducts tax from your income or you pay an amount of tax during the year, you may also have to file a Canadian income tax return to calculate your final tax obligation to Canada.


For more information on your tax obligations, go to:
  • Non-residents of Canada
  • Guide T4002, Self-employed Business, Professional, Commission, Farming, and Fishing Income
  • Disposing of or acquiring certain Canadian property


    • If you need to change an assessed income tax and benefit return to reflect this residency status, go to Changing a tax return.

Factual resident of Canada

  • You have maintained significant residential ties with Canada.
  • You are not considered a resident of another country.
  • You indicated that you are not subject to income tax in another country in the same way as any other - resident of that country.
  • You did not establish significant residential ties in another country.


Note: Do not mail or submit this form to the CRA, unless specifically asked.

Next steps

  1. Print or save this page for your records
  2. Notify the Canada Revenue Agency of your residency status
  3. Prepare your return
    • Make sure to indicate your residency status in the Information about your residency status section of the Income Tax and Benefit Return
    • If you already filed your return without this information, adjust your tax return, go to Changing a tax return.
  4. If necessary, notify any payers in Canada (such as banks, employer, pension payer, and similar) of your change of residence

The term factual resident means that, although you left Canada, you are still considered to be a resident of Canada for income tax purposes.

As a factual resident, your income is taxed as if you never left Canada. As such, you will continue to be subject to tax in Canada on your world income (income from sources both inside and outside Canada).

For each tax year that you’re a factual resident of Canada, use the income tax package for the province or territory where you keep residential ties. Generally, this is where you lived before you left Canada.

You may be able to claim the federal, provincial or territorial foreign tax credit for foreign income or profit taxes that you paid on income you earned outside Canada and reported on your Canadian tax return. Tax treaties with other countries may affect whether you are eligible for this credit. A foreign tax credit can provide you with relief from double taxation on certain income. For more information, refer to Income Tax Folio S5-F2-C1.

For more information on your tax obligations, go to Factual residents – Temporarily outside of Canada
